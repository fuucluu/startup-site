Craft-fair planning guide

Craft fair profit is not revenue. Here is the math that matters.

A busy booth can still lose money. Before paying a vendor fee, calculate what each transaction contributes after product and payment costs, then use that number to find break-even sales and hourly earnings.

Quick answer: subtract the product cost and expected payment fee from your average sale. Divide total fixed event costs by what remains and round up. That is the approximate number of transactions needed to break even.

1. Add every fixed event cost

Fixed event costs are paid even if you make zero sales. Start with the booth or table fee, then add the application fee, travel, parking, food, lodging, permits, insurance, hired help, and event-specific display spending.

Fixed event cost = booth fee + all other event costs

2. Find the contribution from one average sale

Contribution is what one average customer transaction leaves to pay the event's fixed costs and eventually create profit.

Contribution per sale = average sale - product cost - estimated payment fee

If 80% of transactions are expected to be paid by card, apply the card fee only to that expected share. Confirm current fees with your own payment provider instead of copying an old rate from a blog post.

3. Calculate break-even sales

Break-even sales = fixed event cost / contribution per sale, rounded up

Rounding up matters. If the result is 10.2 transactions, the event does not break even until transaction 11.

Worked example

Event assumptions

  • $125 booth fee and $75 in other event costs
  • $28 average sale and $9 product cost per transaction
  • 80% paid by card at 2.6% plus $0.15
  • 30 expected transactions across 10 total hours

The estimated payment fee is about $0.70 per average transaction. Contribution is about $18.30. Fixed cost is $200, so the event needs 11 transactions, or about $308 in revenue, to break even. At 30 transactions, estimated profit is $348.93, or $34.89 per hour.

4. Calculate expected profit and hourly earnings

Expected profit = expected transactions x contribution per sale - fixed event cost

Effective hourly earnings = expected profit / total event hours

Count preparation, loading, travel, setup, selling, teardown, and the trip home. Counting only open-to-public hours makes the result look better than the day really is.

5. Stress-test the forecast

Sales forecasts are uncertain. Weather, event fit, traffic quality, vendor mix, and your product assortment all matter. Recalculate with fewer transactions and a lower average sale. If the event only looks worthwhile in the best case, it is a fragile decision.

When a calculator is not enough

A one-event estimate is a useful filter. A repeatable business workflow also needs product recipes, fee-aware price scenarios, inventory planning, event history, backups, and a comparison between forecast and actual results. Fair Margin connects those steps in one local workflow.
